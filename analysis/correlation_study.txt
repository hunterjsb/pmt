Fire order (what a same-side cap decides on):
  +221s  xrp     loser
  +248s  eth     WINNER

  The FIRST-fired leg here is the LOSER (xrp). A 'keep first-fired,
  cap the rest' same-side cap would have KEPT xrp and BLOCKED the
  winning eth leg. A 'keep last-fired' cap would do the opposite —
  but nothing in the fire order correlates with which leg wins, so
  either rule is a coin flip on a split. The cap cannot tell them
  apart ex ante, because ex ante they are one macro bet.

correlation tail (all five wrong at once, a cap's best case) and 14:05
is the split (a cap forfeits a real winner). S3h prices how often each
shape actually occurs.

--- S3g  WHAT WOULD JUSTIFY A CAP AT ALL (the arithmetic, not the n=2)
  Buy $1 of a side at price p. Win -> +$(1-p)/p. Lose -> -$1. Refusing
  that dollar is +EV exactly when its loss probability q exceeds

      q* = 1 - p

  because (1-q)(1-p)/p - q < 0  <=>  q > 1-p. This is L27's asymmetry
  read forwards: at these prices a blocked winner costs pennies and a
  blocked loser saves the whole dollar, so a cap needs only a SMALL
  excess loss rate to pay. It is also why the hit-rate table above is
  the wrong statistic to decide on.

  fill-weighted entry price on post-theta 5m clips: p = 0.935
  -> break-even loss rate q* = 6.5%

  population                windows   losses  loss rate        vs q*
  1 arms same side               44        0       0.0%        below
  2 arms same side               46        1       2.2%        below
  3 arms same side               36        2       5.6%        below
  4 arms same side +             14        5      35.7%        ABOVE

  So the SHAPE of the case for a cap is sound — >=4-same-side is far
  above break-even. What is missing is n: that row is 3 epochs, one
  of which is the incident the policy was designed after. The
  arithmetic says 'a cap would pay if this loss rate is real'; the
  corpus cannot yet say the loss rate is real. Those are different
  claims and only the second one licenses a deploy.

A same-side PILE is one (epoch, side) with >=2 arms fired, every one of
them graded. Three shapes at settlement:
  unanimous WIN  — every leg won. A cap here forfeits pure profit.

A cap is blind to which shape it is in: ex ante all three look like the
same thing, N arms agreeing on a side.

READ THIS CAREFULLY — it is partly TAUTOLOGICAL and saying so is the

CUSHION — stop issuing `banked_decided` until the settlement TWAP
starts locking — and not a terminal-rule entry signal. Do not confuse
the two: one withholds a false certificate, the other trades on noise.
