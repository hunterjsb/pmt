# 4h up/down tier fit — VERDICT: **DO NOT ARM** (and a live-fleet finding)

**Date** 2026-08-23 · **Commissioned as** ROADMAP Phase 3.2 item 2 (tail-snipe) + R9 (θ gate)
**Tooling** `analysis/fourh_fit.py`, `analysis/fourh_book_snapshot.py`,
`analysis/settlement_rule_check.py`, `analysis/r6_tail_flip_study.py` (now carries `4h`)
**Corpus** 90d × 1m klines, six symbols, gapless (129,600 min each) · 50h Chainlink rounds ·
gamma resolutions for **3,193 resolved windows** · data-api print tape for 24 expired 4h windows
Nothing armed, disarmed, or touched the engine.

---

## TL;DR

The 4h fit was supposed to answer "θ, size, guards". It instead found that **the model the engine
prices with is not the model these markets settle on**, and the 4h tier is where that error becomes
unmissable.

- `eval_model`'s twap branch settles a window on the **average of the whole range** vs the range-start
  reference. The markets actually settle on the **Chainlink 60s TWAP at the range END** vs the same
  stream at the range start. Over 3,193 resolved windows the terminal rule matches resolution
  **98.98% (4h) / 96.53% (15m) / 93.02% (5m)**; the range-average rule manages **86.02 / 85.66 /
  87.57%**. On the 415 windows where the two rules disagree, the terminal rule is right **364 to 51**.
- Consequence for the brief's question: **there is no "banked" mass in these markets.** Nothing is
  ever banked-decided until the final minute, so `banked_decided`, `flip_proof`, `cushion_bp` and the
  R9 safety score all report certainty that does not exist. A 4h arm run exactly as the brief
  proposes (θ=1.0, final-hour entry) would have bought the losing side of **9.6%** of the windows it
  entered (75 of 781, gamma truth) while the model rated them ~100% safe.
- The 4h book is **not** mispriced — it prices the *correct* model closely. The 4–23¢ of "edge" the
  engine sees in a 4h book is the mis-specification, nothing else. Re-pricing 15,000+ real prints
  with the correct model turns an apparent +12¢ to +46¢ feast into a **mean −4.5¢ to +0.3¢ (BTC),
  −10.3¢ to −1.3¢ (ETH), −1.3¢ to +3.9¢ (SOL)** per share.
- **Live-fleet flag (not part of the brief, but urgent):** the same gate approximation over 1,067
  resolved 15m windows would have bought the losing side **13.2%** of the time at θ=0.3 and **7.5%**
  at θ=1.0. At 5m it is **3.5% / 1.8%** — which is why the wallet's 5m record looks fine and its 15m
  record does not. The error scales with window length: it is nearly invisible at 5m, material at
  15m, and disqualifying at 4h.

**Recommendation: do not arm any 4h window. Re-specify `eval_model`'s twap branch first, then re-run
this fit — the tooling is committed and takes minutes.**

## 1. The finding

### 1.1 The ambiguity

Every up/down market carries the same description:

> This market will resolve to "Up" if the time-weighted average price (TWAP) of Bitcoin, generated by
> Chainlink, **of the time range specified in the title** is greater than or equal to **the price at
> the beginning of that range**. […] the BTC/USD TWAP data stream available at
> https://data.chain.link/streams/btc-usd-twap-60s-streams

Two readings. Either (a) the average over the whole range, which is what `eval_model` computes and
what the whole banked/cushion apparatus is built on, or (b) the **60s-TWAP stream read at the end of
the range** — each tick of that stream is itself a 60s average, so "the TWAP of the range" is its
value at the range's end. Identical text at 5m, 15m and 4h.

### 1.2 The test

`analysis/settlement_rule_check.py` scores five candidate readings against gamma's resolved
`outcomePrices` (which only pin to 1/0 after UMA settles), across six symbols and three durations.
The decisive statistic is the head-to-head on the subset where the two readings disagree.

| duration | resolved windows | **terminal-TWAP rule** | range-average rule (`eval_model`) | disagreements | head-to-head |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4h** | 1,080 | **98.98%** | 86.02% | 148 | **144 – 4** (97.3%) |
| **15m** | 1,067 | **96.53%** | 85.66% | 160 | **138 – 22** (86.2%) |
| **5m** | 1,046 | **93.02%** | 87.57% | 107 | **82 – 25** (76.6%) |

The terminal rule's score decays at short durations because the disagreement set there has a median
|margin| of 2.2bp (5m) — inside measured basis noise — whereas at 4h it is 11.1bp, well outside it.
The 4h column is the clean read, and it is nearly unanimous.

Other readings tested and rejected: reference = the range's first minute rather than the minute
before it (`last_vs_firstmin` 98.15% at 4h — close, but strictly worse), and "this range's average vs
the previous range's average" (68.98%).

### 1.3 Confirmation on Chainlink's own data, not Binance's

Two windows from the 50h oracle corpus, computed directly from Chainlink rounds (so no
Binance-vs-Chainlink basis is involved):

| window | Chainlink range-average vs ref | Chainlink terminal-60s vs ref | resolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sol-updown-4h-1787443200` | **+124.7bp** | **−18.8bp** | **Down** |
| `bnb-updown-4h-1787443200` | **+5.2bp** | **−86.0bp** | **Down** |

A 125bp range-average cannot be reconciled with a Down resolution under any basis story. The rule is
terminal, not average.

### 1.4 The codebase already knew — on one side of it

`pmtrader/polymarket/outcomes.py::chainlink_outcome` grades windows with
*"the settlement-shaped TWAP at window end vs the same-width TWAP at window start"* — the terminal
rule, correct. `pmengine/src/strategies/updown_model.rs::eval_model` (twap branch) prices with the
range average. **The grader and the pricer disagree with each other**, and the pricer is the one that
spends money. `pmtrader/polymarket/crypto.py::_model_twap` (the `pmt crypto updown` one-shot) has the
same shape as the engine, so the CLI's verdicts inherit the error too.

## 2. What this does to the 4h tail-harvest

The brief's premise — "with the theta entry gate live, a 4h arm only ever commits when the window is
genuinely banked-decided" — **cannot hold**, because under the real rule no mass is ever banked.
A 4h window is a four-hour digital option on where the price *ends up*; three and a half hours of
accumulated average tell you almost nothing that the current price doesn't already say.

Engine-gate impact, first clip per window, scored against gamma resolutions:

| duration | θ=0.3: windows entered / lost | θ=1.0 (`banked_decided`): entered / lost |
|---|---|---|
| **4h** | 888 → **148 lost (16.7%)** | 781 → **75 lost (9.6%)** |
| 15m | 793 → 105 lost (13.2%) | 710 → 53 lost (7.5%) |
| 5m | 458 → 16 lost (3.5%) | 382 → 7 lost (1.8%) |

And the same thing measured the other way, on the 90d kline corpus, for the exact posture the brief
proposed (θ=1.0, first clip only inside the final 60 minutes):

| symbol | fires/day/arm | win% the model believes (range-average) | **win% under the real rule** |
|---|---|---|---|
| BTC | 5.03 | 99.8% | **86.1%** |
| ETH | 4.83 | 100.0% | **89.6%** |
| SOL | 5.02 | 99.8% | **88.2%** |
| XRP | 4.00 | 100.0% | **93.6%** |
| DOGE | 4.37 | 100.0% | **89.8%** |
| BNB | 4.83 | 99.5% | **88.9%** |

At a typical harvest entry of ~0.95 (fee ≈0.35¢), a 12% loss rate gives
`0.88 × 4.65¢ − 0.12 × 95¢ = −7.3¢ per share` ≈ **−$11.5 per $150 window**, ~5 windows/day/arm ≈
**−$58/day/arm**. Three arms ≈ −$175/day. That is the size of the trap the brief was walking into,
and every safety rail would have been *disabled* on the way in: θ=1.0 means `banked_decided`, which
exempts the clip from the book-distrust brake (the 4h book routinely shows >15¢ of fake net) and from
the no-averaging-down brake, unlocks the full budget, and — per R7's design — exempts the arm from
the fleet cap.

**The gate that was supposed to make 4h safe is exactly the gate that would have removed every
other protection.**

## 3. What it does to the live fleet

Not the brief's question, but it falls out of the same measurement and it is live right now.

- **5m (btc5m, eth5m, sol5m): 3.5% wrong-side rate at θ=0.3, 1.8% at θ=1.0.** Consistent with the
  wallet's 30-1 record. The two rules almost always agree over five minutes, so the mis-specification
  is close to harmless here.
- **15m (btc15m, eth15m, sol15m): 13.2% at θ=0.3, 7.5% at θ=1.0.** This is material. At the 0.90–0.98
  entry prices these arms pay, a 13% loss rate is negative expectancy. It also offers a simpler
  explanation for the wallet's 15m record (5-1 including the −$370 window) than "a sizing/brake
  failure" — R4's open contradiction may be a pricing bug, not an allocation question.
- The R6 study's headline ("conditional flip rate at safety ≥ 1.0 is ~0% in every bucket") was
  measured against the range-average definition, which the study's own docstring is explicit about.
  It is a true statement about a quantity that does not settle anything. R9's replay validation
  (11-0) used wallet truth and stands, but it is 11 windows of 5m/15m btc/eth — the regime where the
  two rules agree.

These are gate approximations (first tick where the guard + θ pass, no book/edge constraint), not a
wallet replay. The right follow-up is a `pmengine replay` A/B of the corrected model against the
recorded nights, which the harness can already do.

## 5. The five questions, answered as asked

**Q1 — Evidence timeline.** Under the range-average model (the one the brief assumed), safety first
crosses θ=0.3 at a median 100 minutes elapsed (42% of the window), θ=1.0 at 160 minutes (66%);
76–84% of 4h windows ever reach θ=1.0 and 64–75% do so before the final 30 minutes; fires/day/arm at
θ=0.3/0.5/1.0 = 5.6/5.4/5.1 (BTC) down to 4.7/4.4/4.0 (XRP). **These numbers are arithmetically
correct and strategically meaningless** — they describe the arrival of evidence about a quantity that
does not decide the market. The honest version of Q1 is §2's table: the same gate, scored against
what actually settles, wins 86–93% of the time.

**Q2 — Flip safety at 4h scale.** Two answers, and the second dominates.
- *Within the model's own frame*, the cushion behaves well: conditional flip rate at θ=1.0 is
  0.0–0.46% across symbols, all residual flips coming from entries with >1h left (0 flips in ~800
  entries inside the final hour). σ√(T/3) holds with the centre conservative — realised residual
  scale is 0.5–0.9σ of the claim at every horizon — while the tails do fatten with horizon (|z|>3 at
  2.35% for rem>2h vs 0.3% for rem<15m; |z|>5 at 0.50% vs 0.02%). So multi-hour drifts *are* more
  non-Gaussian than 15m paths, but the cushion is scaled conservatively enough to absorb it.
- *Against reality*, that entire frame is the wrong measurement. The real flip rate at θ=1.0 in the
  final hour is 6.4–13.9% (§2), not 0%.
- **Margins:** median |final margin| at 4h is 19.8 / 25.9 / 32.1 / 29.6 / 31.9 / 21.9bp
  (btc/eth/sol/xrp/doge/bnb) versus 5.4 / 7.0 / 8.5 / 7.7 / 8.1 / 5.9bp at 15m — **3.7× fatter**, with
  35–52% of 4h windows finishing ≥30bp out and the guard disqualifying only 17–33% of 4h windows vs
  54–79% at 15m. So the brief's structural intuition was right: *if* the range-average were what
  settled, 4h would be proportionally far less exposed to basis (measured P(flip|gate) 0.05–0.36% at
  4h vs 0.15–0.88% at 15m, a 2–4× improvement, from convolving the measured 50h Chainlink error with
  the 90d margin distribution). It does not rescue the tier, because the dominant error is not basis —
  it is the settlement definition, which is 10–100× larger.
- **Does this overturn R1's XRP/DOGE no-trade verdict?** On the basis leg alone, at 4h, it would have:
  XRP 0.28% and DOGE 0.34% P(flip|gate) with 17/14bp guards are *better* than SOL 15m's 0.71% and
  ETH 15m's 0.48%, and those guards block only 33%/28% of 4h windows instead of 79%/70% — R1's
  "a 20-25bp guard guts the edge" is a 15m statement that does not transfer to 4h. **But it is moot**:
  the tier fails for a reason that has nothing to do with basis, and XRP/DOGE 4h print $1.4k/$0.18k of
  decided-side volume per final hour anyway. R1's verdict stands, for a better reason than R1 gave.

**Q3 — Late-window book quality.** §4. Plus the liquidity facts, which survive the finding intact:

| symbol | 4h window volume | final-60m | final-60m decided-side (median) | prints in final 60m | gamma `liquidity` at T-53m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BTC | $20k–51k | $3.1k–18.7k | ~$8.0k | 184–532 | $5,754 |
| ETH | $8k–26k | $1.4k–7.8k | ~$3.4k | 89–348 | $4,287 |
| SOL | $2.9k–12.4k | $0.7k–7.9k | ~$1.3k | 53–630 | $4,193 |
| XRP | $3.2k–60.8k | $0.8k–8.1k | ~$1.4k | 53–300 | $2,981 |
| DOGE | $0.35k–1.1k | $0.07k–0.5k | ~$0.18k | 6–52 | $2,528 |
| BNB | $0.27k–1.7k | $0.02k–1.2k | ~$0.26k | 5–53 | $1,949 |

15–81% of a 4h window's volume prints in its final hour, so the tape *is* concentrated where a
tail-snipe would want it. Top-of-book size in the snapshot was $1–75 with $142–570 across three
levels — $25 clips are the right granularity if this tier is ever revisited. DOGE and BNB cannot
absorb a $150 arm without being the tape.

**Q4 — Interaction with the fleet.** Measured (90d, 60s grid, θ=0.3): a 4h gate is open 36–45% of all
minutes (16–20% under a final-hour posture); when it is, the same-symbol 15m gate is open 13–25% of
the time and agrees on side **49–57%** — a coin flip with a ≤7pp tilt, confirmed by the marginal
up-shares (46–50% vs 48–50%). Each window measures its own TWAP against its own reference, so ρ≈1 on
the price path does *not* imply correlated verdicts. That result is about model states and survives
the finding only as a statement about the *mis-specified* gate; under the corrected model both
durations reduce to "is spot far from this window's reference", which should correlate **more**, since
a 4h window's reference is one of the 15m references. Recommended fleet posture is therefore moot for
now: **no 4h arms**, and the R7 cap argument needs re-deriving once the model is fixed, because
`banked_decided` — the flag that exempts an arm from the cap — is exactly the flag that is
unreliable.

**Q5 — Verdict and parameters.** No symbol's 4h is armable today. Not θ=1.0, not final-30m, not at
$50. The parameters that *would* matter after a fix are in §6.

## 6. What a correct 4h tail-snipe would need

The corrected model is not new code — it is the shape `eval_model` already implements in its
`close_open` branch:

```
ref   = 60s TWAP at range start        (the mark of the minute BEFORE start — unchanged, validated)
S_T   = 60s TWAP at range end          (the settlement quantity)
τ     = max(end − now − 30s, ...)      (the last 30s is already inside the averaging window)
p_up  = Φ( ln(spot/ref) / (σ·√τ) )
```

`analysis/fourh_fit.py::terminal_state` implements it for the study. What changes downstream:

- `banked_margin_bp`, `cushion_bp`, `banked_decided`, `flip_proof` and R9's `safety` all lose their
  meaning; the natural replacement for "how decided is this?" is **|z| = |ln(spot/ref)| / (σ√τ)**,
  which is dimensionally the same kind of score θ was gating on and can reuse the R9 plumbing.
- The basis guard still applies, on |spot − ref| rather than on a projected average.
- Everything the guard/brake/cap machinery keys off `banked_decided` needs re-pointing at the new
  score — that is the risky part of the change and it wants a replay A/B, one small-size night, then
  full size, exactly as the roadmap requires.
- Expect far fewer "certain" windows: at T-60min with BTC σ≈4bp/min, σ√60 ≈ 31bp, so |z|≥3 needs the
  price ~93bp from the reference. The tier's real fire rate under a correct model will be a fraction
  of the 5/day this study measured.

Then re-run this fit. If §4's numbers hold (book at or through true fair), the answer stays "no
4h" — and that is a perfectly good outcome, cheaply obtained.

## 7. Recommendations

1. **Do not arm any 4h window.** No θ, no min_elapsed, no size makes it safe today.
2. **Treat the twap mis-specification as the top item.** It is live on six arms right now. The 5m
   arms are nearly unaffected (1.8–3.5%); the **15m arms carry a 7.5–13.2% wrong-side rate** and are
   the first thing to look at. A defensible interim posture — operator's call, not mine — is to hold
   the 5m arms and pause or shrink the 15m arms until the replay A/B is in.
3. **Fix and validate:** re-specify `eval_model`'s twap branch (and `crypto.py::_model_twap`, which
   feeds `pmt crypto updown` and every arm's sigma), re-point the θ/brake/cap flags at the new score,
   A/B it on the recorded corpus with `--outcomes` wallet truth, one small-size night, then full size.
4. **Re-run R6 and the R9 θ sweep afterwards.** Both were scored against the range-average definition;
   their conclusions need re-deriving, not just re-checking.
5. **Add the rule check to the weekly cadence** beside `pmt crypto basis --aligned`:
   `uv run python ../analysis/settlement_rule_check.py --durations 4h --back 60` is a two-minute
   canary that would catch any future change to how these markets settle.

## 8. Method, reproduction, caveats

```bash
cd pmtrader && uv run python ../analysis/settlement_rule_check.py --durations 4h 15m 5m --back 180
cd pmtrader && uv run python ../analysis/fourh_fit.py                     # 90d, six symbols
cd pmtrader && uv run python ../analysis/fourh_fit.py --limit-days 21     # regime subset
cd pmtrader && uv run python ../analysis/fourh_book_snapshot.py --back 6  # books + print tape
cd pmtrader && uv run python ../analysis/fourh_book_snapshot.py \
    --skip-live --skip-expired --gated-harvest --back 24 --theta 1.0      # §4's print table
```

- `eval_model` is transcribed 1:1 from `updown_model.rs` (2026-08-23), with `r6_tail_flip_study.py`'s
  simplifications: spot = the in-progress minute's (o+c)/2; the arm-time σ fallback approximated by
  the 12m fast window; banked lag a flat now−30s exactly as deployed.
- Two "winners" are computed everywhere: the range-average one (what the model believes) and the
  terminal one (what settles). Tables label which is which.
- Ground truth for §1–§3 is gamma's resolved `outcomePrices`, not klines. The kline-derived terminal
  rule agrees with it 98.98% at 4h; the residual is Binance-vs-Chainlink basis on thin margins.
- Engine-gate impact numbers are **gate approximations** — first tick where the basis guard and θ
  pass, with no book, edge, brake-latch or `min_fair` constraint, and one entry per window. They are
  not a wallet replay and will differ from realised P&L; they are directionally sound and were
  computed identically across durations, which is what the comparison rests on.
- The print-tape sections cover 24 expired 4h windows (four days) — recent and small. The 90d kline
  sections cover 539 4h windows per symbol.
- The 4h corpus was extended from 21d to 90d for this study (21d holds only 125 4h windows per
  symbol). The 21d subset agrees with the 90d headline within ~2pp on every cell.
